**Collaboration:** [Ginger Egberts (Hasselt University)](https://www.uhasselt.be/nl/wie-is-wie/detail/ginger-egberts), [Ibrahim Korkmaz, PhD (Amsterdam UMC)](https://research.vumc.nl/en/persons/ibrahim-korkmaz), [Fred Vermolen (Hasselt University)](https://www.uhasselt.be/nl/wie-is-wie/fred-vermolen), and [Paul van Zuijlen (Amsterdam UMC)](https://research.vumc.nl/en/persons/paul-van-zuijlen)

The project studies computational models of skin evolution after severe burn injuries, with the aim of improving wound-care assessment. Such models help investigate hypertrophic scarring and the large contractions that can follow burn trauma.

Existing models use coupled nonlinear partial differential equations solved with finite elements. Patient-specific and uncertain parameters require many simulations to compare possible outcomes, making direct finite element computation expensive.

The project aims to develop an efficient computational framework that reproduces these simulations, supporting probabilistic comparisons of treatment scenarios.

**Project period:** 1 January 2023–31 December 2026 ([Hasselt University project record](https://www.uhasselt.be/en/projects/detail/26843-project-r-14507)).
